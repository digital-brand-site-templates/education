Ten things to do in your first week on campus

Advice from three years of asking new students what surprised them.

July 17, 2026by Kenny Brown

Every September we ask new students what surprised them most about their first week. The answers are rarely about lectures. They are about finding a seat in the library, working out which café takes student cards and realising it is fine to ask for help.

What did we expect in the first place?

Most of our first-years told us they expected university to feel like school with bigger classrooms. What they found instead was more freedom and less structure: a timetable with gaps in it, reading lists without deadlines attached and tutors who wait to be asked rather than chase. The students who settled fastest were the ones who filled those gaps on purpose, with a regular library slot, a club or a part-time shift.

"I wish someone had told me that office hours are not only for people who are struggling. My tutor was glad I came, and I went every fortnight after that."

So here is our advice, collected from three years of answers. Get your student card in the first two days. Walk the campus once with a map. Go to every introductory seminar, even the optional ones, because that is where you meet people on your course. And book one meeting with your personal tutor before the end of October, whatever else is going on.
